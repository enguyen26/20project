Capri Travel Guide

Capri, Campania, Italy • Updated January 2025

Capri has been a playground for the rich and famous since Roman emperors built villas here, and it hasn't exactly shaken that reputation. This tiny limestone island in the Bay of Naples is stunning—dramatic cliffs, turquoise waters, whitewashed buildings dripping with bougainvillea—but it's also expensive, crowded, and slightly absurd. Day-trippers flood in by the thousands during summer, luxury yachts fill the marina, and a simple lunch can cost more than your ferry ticket.

But here's the thing: if you stay overnight after the day-trippers leave, or visit during shoulder season, you'll understand why everyone from ancient Romans to modern celebrities has been obsessed with this place. The Blue Grotto really is that blue. The views from Monte Solaro genuinely take your breath away. And walking through the quiet streets of Anacapri at sunset, you might forget about the prices and the crowds and just appreciate being on one of the Mediterranean's most beautiful islands.

Just know what you're signing up for: Capri is not an undiscovered gem. It's a famous, expensive, tourist-packed island that happens to be absolutely gorgeous. Go in with realistic expectations and a healthy budget.

Where to Stay in Capri

The island has two main towns: Capri (the main town, not the island—confusing, I know) and Anacapri up on the hillside. Where you stay changes your entire experience.

Capri town is where the action is. You're a short walk from the Piazzetta (the famous main square), the Gardens of Augustus, and Via Camerelle shopping. Hotels here put you in the heart of everything, which means proximity to restaurants and the ferry port, but also crowds and higher prices. The Grand Hotel Quisisana is iconic if you've got the budget. Otherwise, smaller boutique hotels offer the location without the celebrity clientele.

Anacapri is quieter, less expensive, and more residential. You're closer to the chairlift up Monte Solaro and Villa San Michele. The bus connects you to Capri town in 10 minutes, but you'll trade convenience for a more authentic feel and fewer tourists. This is where I'd stay if I were visiting again.

Day trip or overnight? Here's the real question. A day trip from Naples, Sorrento, or Positano is completely doable—you can see the main sights in 6-8 hours. But staying overnight lets you experience the island without the crowds, watch sunset from quieter vantage points, and not feel rushed. If you can afford it, stay at least one night.

Where to Eat

Dining in Capri requires managing expectations. You're on a small island where everything is imported by boat. Prices reflect that. The restaurants around the Piazzetta charge premium rates for mediocre food and people-watching. But there are better options if you look beyond the obvious tourist spots.

For lunch with a view: Da Gelsomina in Anacapri serves traditional dishes with spectacular views and reasonable prices (by Capri standards). L'Approdo down by Marina Piccola offers fresh seafood right by the water without the tourist-trap feel.

For dinner: Il Riccio beach club at the base of the Blue Grotto serves exceptional seafood in a casual setting. Book ahead. In town, Aurora has been around since 1936 and still serves solid traditional food—it's a local favorite despite the celebrity photos on the walls.

For aperitivo: Skip the overpriced cocktails in the Piazzetta and head to the terrace at Capri Rooftop or the Bar Tiberio. Better drinks, similar views, fewer tourists trying to take selfies with their Aperol Spritzes.

Budget option: Get supplies at the markets in Anacapri and have a picnic. A bottle of local wine, fresh bread, cheese, and tomatoes costs a fraction of restaurant prices and tastes just as good with a view.

What to Do

The Blue Grotto is the island's most famous attraction and it's genuinely incredible—sunlight filtering through underwater cavities turns the entire cave electric blue. But getting there is a production. You take a boat from Marina Grande, wait in line (sometimes for an hour), then transfer to tiny rowboats that duck through a low entrance. The whole experience takes 5 minutes inside the cave and costs €18. Is it worth it? Yes, if you have the time and patience. No, if crowds and waiting stress you out.

Monte Solaro chairlift in Anacapri is the best 12 minutes you'll spend on the island. The single-person chairlift slowly carries you up to the island's highest point with increasingly dramatic views. At the top, you can see the entire Bay of Naples, the Amalfi Coast, and on clear days, even Vesuvius. Go in the late afternoon when the light is better.

Gardens of Augustus offer the classic postcard view of the Faraglioni rocks and Via Krupp zigzagging down the cliff (note: Via Krupp is usually closed due to rockfall, but the gardens alone are worth the admission). Early morning or late afternoon avoids the worst crowds.

Walk to Villa Jovis for ruins of Emperor Tiberius's palace and dramatic cliff views without the crowds. It's a 45-minute uphill walk from the Piazzetta, which filters out most tourists. The ruins aren't spectacular, but the location is stunning.

Villa San Michele in Anacapri is a beautiful villa-turned-museum with peaceful gardens and sweeping views. It's quieter than most Capri attractions and gives you a sense of what drew artists and writers to the island.

Via Camerelle shopping is window shopping at its most absurd. Gucci, Prada, Hermès—the same luxury brands you can find anywhere, but here they're on a tiny island reached only by boat. The street itself is beautiful though, even if you're not buying €2,000 sandals.

My Favorite Spots

  • Monte Solaro chairlift for the best views on the island
  • Anacapri in the evening after day-trippers leave
  • Early morning Piazzetta before 9am when it's actually peaceful
  • Gardens of Augustus for the classic Capri view
  • Il Riccio beach club for lunch by the water
  • Walking the quiet streets away from the main shopping areas
  • Watching ferries depart from Marina Grande around 5pm

Practical Tips

Getting there: Ferries run from Naples (50 minutes), Sorrento (25 minutes), and Positano (40 minutes). Multiple companies operate the routes—Caremar and NLG are reliable. Book online in advance during peak season (June-September) as ferries sell out. The hydrofoil is faster but more expensive than the regular ferry. Both arrive at Marina Grande.

Getting around: The funicular connects Marina Grande to Capri town in 5 minutes. From there, buses run to Anacapri. Everything else is walking. Taxis and private cars exist but are expensive and unnecessary—the island is small. Forget about driving here yourself; private cars aren't allowed except for residents.

When to visit: May, June, September, and October offer the best balance of weather and fewer crowds. July and August are absolute chaos—ferries packed, restaurants full, prices at their highest. Avoid August entirely if possible. Winter (November-March) is quiet and many businesses close, but if you want Capri without tourists, this is your window.

Timing within the day: Day-trippers typically arrive around 10am and leave by 5pm. If you're staying overnight, those evening hours are magical. If you're day-tripping, arrive on the earliest ferry and leave on the latest one to avoid the worst crowds.

Money: Everything is expensive. A simple cappuccino in the Piazzetta costs €7-10. Beach club loungers are €30-50 per person. Budget accordingly. Cards are accepted most places, but keep some cash for smaller vendors and boat trips.

What to skip: The tourist shops selling ceramic lemons and "Capri" labeled products are identical to shops on the Amalfi Coast. The beaches aren't great—mostly pebbles and concrete platforms. If you want beach time, you're better off in Positano or elsewhere on the coast.

Crowds: There's no avoiding them completely in summer, but staying overnight, visiting in shoulder season, and hitting major sights early or late makes a huge difference. The Blue Grotto is least crowded first thing in the morning (weather permitting).
