Grindelwald Travel Guide

Grindelwald, Bernese Oberland, Switzerland • Updated January 2025

Grindelwald is the postcard. The iconic Swiss mountain village with chalets scattered across green meadows, the Eiger North Face looming overhead, and hiking trails that lead to views you've seen in a thousand photos. It's touristy, yes—very touristy. But when you wake up to those mountains, when you step out of your hotel and see the scale of the peaks surrounding you, the crowds start to make sense.

This is the Bernese Oberland's most accessible mountain resort, which is both its strength and weakness. The infrastructure is flawless—gondolas, trains, hotels, restaurants—all operating with Swiss precision. But it comes at a cost, both literally (this place is expensive even by Swiss standards) and atmospherically (you're sharing those Instagram-worthy views with everyone else). Still, if you're coming to Switzerland for mountains, Grindelwald delivers exactly what you came for.

Getting There and Around

Grindelwald is about 2-3 hours from Zurich by train (change in Interlaken). The journey itself is stunning, especially the stretch from Interlaken up to the mountains. Once you're here, everything is walkable in the village center, but you'll rely on mountain railways and gondolas to reach the high-altitude attractions.

The Jungfrau Travel Pass covers most transport in the region if you're doing multiple excursions. Otherwise, individual tickets are expensive—budget accordingly.

What to Do

First Cliff Walk: Take the gondola to First, then walk the suspended metal platform that juts out over the cliff edge. It's dramatic, vertiginous, and completely safe (this is Switzerland). The views across the valley to the Wetterhorn are exceptional. You can hike down or take the gondola back. In winter, this area is a ski resort.

Hiking: Summer is hiking season. Trails range from easy valley walks to serious alpine routes. The Eiger Trail (from Eigergletscher to Alpiglen) offers close-up views of the North Face. For something easier, walk from Grindelwald to Kleine Scheidegg with the peaks all around you. Pick up maps from the tourist office.

Jungfraujoch: The "Top of Europe" is accessible from Grindelwald via Kleine Scheidegg (see separate Jungfraujoch guide). It's a bucket-list experience but expensive and crowded. Book ahead and go early morning.

Pfingstegg: A short cable car ride from the village takes you to this less-crowded viewpoint. There's an alpine coaster here (surprisingly fun) and access to hiking trails leading to the Obere Gletscher gorge.

Where to Stay

Accommodation in Grindelwald is expensive. A decent hotel runs CHF 200-300+ per night in peak season. Budget hotels and hostels exist but book early—they fill up fast.

Stay in the village center for convenience—you're walking distance to restaurants, shops, and the train station. The tradeoff is noise from other tourists and higher prices.

Stay uphill (toward First or Männlichen) for better views and quieter evenings. You'll walk or take a short bus ride to reach the center, but waking up to unobstructed mountain views makes it worth it.

Consider staying in nearby Lauterbrunnen instead—it's cheaper, less touristy, and only 20 minutes away by train. You'll get a more authentic alpine village feel without sacrificing access to the region.

Where to Eat

Restaurants here cater to tourists, with prices to match. Expect CHF 20-30 for a basic meal, CHF 40-60 for anything sit-down.

For traditional Swiss food: Try C&M Cafe Bar or Restaurant Barry's for fondue, rösti, and raclette. The food is solid, the portions are generous, and you'll pay for the privilege.

For better value: Head to the Coop supermarket in the village center. Their prepared food section is a lifesaver when you're tired of restaurant prices. Grab sandwiches, salads, or hot food, and eat with a view somewhere outside.

Mountain restaurants: If you're hiking or skiing, the mountain huts serve hearty alpine food—think sausages, soups, and beer. Prices are high (you're paying to transport everything up), but the atmosphere is authentic.

Practical Tips

Timing: Summer (June-September) is best for hiking, with wildflowers in July. Winter (December-March) is ski season—expensive and crowded but magical under snow. Avoid late April to early June when many mountain lifts close for maintenance.

Weather: Mountain weather changes fast. Bring layers, waterproof jacket, and check forecasts before committing to expensive excursions. If Jungfraujoch is clouded in, don't waste your money—reschedule.

Costs: Grindelwald is expensive. Budget CHF 150-200 per person per day minimum for accommodation, food, and mountain transport. A Jungfraujoch ticket alone costs CHF 200+. The Swiss Travel Pass offers discounts but doesn't eliminate the sticker shock.

Crowds: Peak season (July-August, Christmas-New Year) sees massive tour groups, especially from Asia. First and Jungfraujoch get packed. Visit in June or September for better weather and fewer crowds.

It's touristy: Accept this going in. Grindelwald isn't a hidden gem—it's Switzerland's most famous mountain village. But the mountains are real, the scenery is extraordinary, and Swiss efficiency means everything works smoothly even when it's busy. Embrace the tourism, enjoy the infrastructure, and marvel at the peaks.
