Bernese Oberland Travel Guide

Bernese Oberland, Switzerland • Updated January 2025

The Bernese Oberland is Switzerland's most dramatic alpine region—a landscape of vertical peaks, hanging glaciers, and valleys so steep you crane your neck looking up at them. This is where you'll find the Eiger, Mönch, and Jungfrau mountains, the valley of 72 waterfalls, and some of the most famous mountain scenery in Europe. It's also where Switzerland's tourism machine operates at full power, with pristine villages, efficient trains, and prices to match the altitude.

The region centers around Interlaken, which sits between two lakes and serves as the gateway to everything else. But the real highlights are up in the mountains—Lauterbrunnen's waterfall valley, Grindelwald's Eiger views, the Jungfraujoch "Top of Europe" railway, and the quieter corners like Iseltwald on Lake Brienz. This guide covers the best of the Bernese Oberland, where to base yourself, and how to see it without bankrupting yourself (though Switzerland will try).

Lauterbrunnen - The Valley Base

Lauterbrunnen is the valley that makes everyone stop and stare. A deep U-shaped valley with cliffs rising 300 meters on both sides, waterfalls tumbling from every direction, and tiny villages perched impossibly high on the valley walls. It's dramatic, beautiful, and surprisingly functional—this isn't a remote wilderness, it's a working alpine community with excellent infrastructure, good hotels, and train connections to everywhere you want to go in the region.

Where Grindelwald is the famous resort town, Lauterbrunnen is the quieter base—more affordable, less crowded, and arguably more scenic. The valley serves as the gateway to Mürren, Wengen, and Jungfraujoch, but it's worth staying here for the waterfalls alone.

The Waterfalls

Staubbach Falls is the icon—a 300-meter waterfall dropping straight down from the cliff edge into the village. You can walk right up to the base of it from the town center in about 10 minutes. In summer, it's a thin veil of water that catches the light. In spring (snowmelt), it's more dramatic.

Trümmelbach Falls are the hidden gem—waterfalls inside the mountain, accessed by tunnels and stairs. The sheer volume of water carving through solid rock is mesmerizing. It's touristy (entry costs CHF 11) but genuinely impressive. The falls drain the glaciers of Eiger, Mönch, and Jungfrau—up to 20,000 liters per second.

Beyond these two, there are 70 other waterfalls scattered throughout the valley. You can see many from the train or while hiking. The valley itself is the attraction.

Mürren and Wengen

Mürren: Take the cable car up to this car-free village on the cliff edge. The views are spectacular, and it's the starting point for the Schilthorn cable car (James Bond filming location). Even if you don't go all the way up, Mürren itself is worth visiting for lunch and a wander.

Wengen: On the opposite side of the valley, accessible by train. Another car-free alpine village with hiking trails and mountain access. Less dramatic than Mürren but equally charming.

Grindelwald - The Mountain Village

Grindelwald is Switzerland's most famous mountain resort, sitting at the base of the Eiger North Face with Instagram-perfect views in every direction. It's beautiful, efficient, and expensive—this is resort Switzerland at its most polished. The Eiger dominates the skyline, cable cars whisk you to viewpoints, and well-marked hiking trails connect everything.

But Grindelwald is also crowded and touristy. Summer brings hiking crowds, winter brings skiers, and year-round the village feels more like a theme park than an authentic mountain town. That said, the access to hiking and mountain activities is genuinely excellent, and if you can afford it, this is a comfortable base for exploring the region.

What to Do

First Cliff Walk: Take the gondola to First, then walk the suspended cliff walkway with dramatic drops and Eiger views. It's touristy but genuinely thrilling. The area offers excellent hiking, including the trail down to Bachalpsee (a mountain lake that reflects the peaks on calm days).

Eiger Trail: This moderate hike takes you along the base of the Eiger North Face. The scale of the wall is impossible to appreciate until you're standing beneath it. Allow 2-3 hours.

Pfingstegg: A smaller cable car takes you to this area with a toboggan run (summer and winter) and hiking trails. Less crowded than First but still scenic.

Jungfraujoch - Top of Europe

Jungfraujoch is the highest railway station in Europe at 3,454 meters, accessed by a cogwheel train that climbs through the heart of the Eiger and Mönch mountains. It's expensive (CHF 200+ round-trip from Interlaken), crowded, and aggressively marketed as the "Top of Europe." But on a clear day, standing on the glacier with 360-degree views of the Alps, it's genuinely spectacular.

The reality: You're paying for the experience and the views, not for authentic alpine adventure. The summit complex includes viewing platforms, an ice palace carved into the glacier, restaurants, and gift shops. It's a managed experience designed for tour groups. But the Aletsch Glacier (the longest in the Alps) stretching out below is breathtaking, and the altitude—you're higher than most Alpine peaks—feels significant.

Is It Worth It?

If you're in the Bernese Oberland and weather is good, yes. If it's cloudy or you're on a tight budget, skip it. The train ride is scenic, but you're paying CHF 200 to potentially stare at fog. Check mountain webcams before committing.

Tips: Go early (first train) to beat crowds. Bring warm clothes (it's cold at 3,454m even in summer). Altitude can cause headaches—take it slow. The Swiss Travel Pass gives a 25% discount, and the Good Morning Ticket (before 8am) is slightly cheaper.

Iseltwald - The Lakeside Village

Iseltwald is a tiny village on the southern shore of Lake Brienz that became Instagram-famous after appearing in the Netflix K-drama "Crash Landing on You." Before 2020, it was a quiet lakeside hamlet. Now it's overrun with tourists—mostly Korean drama fans—who come for selfies at the iconic pier and then leave.

The reality: Iseltwald has one famous pier, one hotel (the Strand Hotel where the drama was filmed), a handful of houses, and stunning views across the turquoise waters of Lake Brienz. That's it. There's no town center, no restaurants worth mentioning, and very little to do beyond taking photos. It's picturesque in the extreme, but the fame has turned it into a photo stop rather than a destination.

Should you visit? If you're a "Crash Landing on You" fan, it's a pilgrimage worth making. If you're not, spend your time in Brienz, Lauterbrunnen, or one of the region's many other stunning locations that haven't been Instagram-ified into oblivion. Visit for 30-60 minutes, take your photos, and move on.

Where to Stay in the Region

Lauterbrunnen is my top choice for a base. It's cheaper than Grindelwald, more scenic, and has excellent train connections to everywhere in the region. Stay here and day-trip to Grindelwald, not the other way around. Hotels run CHF 100-200 per night. Camping Jungfrau offers budget camping with incredible views.

Grindelwald works if you want resort amenities and don't mind paying for them. It's more expensive but offers more dining options and nightlife (by Swiss village standards). Hotels start at CHF 150-250 per night.

Interlaken is the gateway town between the two lakes. It's less scenic than the mountain villages but offers better value accommodations and easy access to everything. Good for budget travelers or those who want to be centrally located.

Mürren or Wengen offer car-free alpine charm with sunrise mountain views. Both are more expensive and require cable cars or trains to reach, but the atmosphere is special. Best if you want to wake up in the mountains.

Where to Eat

Eating in the Bernese Oberland is expensive. Restaurant meals easily cost CHF 25-40 per person. Your best strategy is to stock up at Coop or Migros supermarkets in the valley towns for breakfast and lunch, then splurge on one nice dinner.

In Lauterbrunnen: Hotel Oberland Restaurant serves traditional Swiss fare at reasonable (by Swiss standards) prices—CHF 20-35 for meals. Airtime Cafe offers burgers and pizzas with better prices. The Coop supermarket is your budget-friendly option.

In Grindelwald: Restaurants are pricier but offer more variety. C und M Cafe has good sandwiches and lighter meals. For fondue, try Restaurant Glacier—touristy but authentic. Meals run CHF 30-50.

Mountain restaurants: Expect to pay CHF 15-20 for a basic meal at mountain huts and cable car stations. Bring your own snacks for hiking to save money.

Practical Information

Timing: Visit in late spring (May-June) when snowmelt makes waterfalls most dramatic, or summer (July-September) for hiking. Avoid April when trails may still be snowy. Winter is beautiful under snow but many hiking trails close.

Costs: Budget CHF 100-150 per day for accommodation, food, and local transport. Cable cars and mountain excursions cost extra—Schilthorn roundtrip is CHF 100+, Jungfraujoch CHF 200+. A Swiss Travel Pass (CHF 200+ for 3 days) makes sense if you're doing multiple excursions.

Weather: Mountain weather changes fast. Check webcams before expensive excursions. Valleys can be cloudy while peaks are clear, or vice versa. Bring layers and rain gear always.

Transportation: Trains and cable cars connect everything efficiently. Use the SBB app for schedules. The Swiss Travel Pass covers most trains but only gives discounts (25-50%) on mountain cable cars. Day passes for specific areas can be good value.

Crowds: July-August is peak season with maximum crowds and prices. Shoulder seasons (May-June, September-October) offer better weather-to-crowd ratios. Winter (December-March) is skiing season.

A Few of My Favorite Spots

  • Staubbach and Trümmelbach waterfalls in Lauterbrunnen
  • Mürren village and Schilthorn cable car
  • First Cliff Walk and Bachalpsee hike near Grindelwald
  • Eiger Trail along the North Face
  • Jungfraujoch on a clear day (check webcams first)
  • Valley hiking trails away from cable car stations
  • Camping Jungfrau for budget mountain views
  • Early morning before tour groups arrive

Final Thoughts

The Bernese Oberland is expensive, crowded, and sometimes overwhelming in its efficiency and commercialization. But the mountains are genuinely spectacular. The infrastructure makes it accessible without requiring serious alpine experience. And despite the crowds, there are still quiet moments—early morning in Lauterbrunnen before the tour buses arrive, a sunset hike above Grindelwald, or sitting by the lake in Brienz watching the light change on the peaks.

Use Lauterbrunnen as your base, do one big splurge (Jungfraujoch or Schilthorn on a clear day), spend the rest of your time hiking and exploring the valleys, and accept that you're going to spend more money than planned. That's the Bernese Oberland—worth it, but you'll feel it in your wallet.
