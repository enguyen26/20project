Paris Travel Guide

Île-de-France, France • Updated January 2025

With its iconic landmarks, world-class museums, and charming neighborhoods, Paris is so much more than just the Eiffel Tower.

Over multiple trips to Paris, I've learned the city is best enjoyed slowly. Take time to wander through different neighborhoods, sit at cafés, and eat well. The major sights are worth seeing, but my favorite Paris moments happen in quiet corners—morning croissants at neighborhood bakeries, afternoons in parks, evenings at wine bars.

Where to Stay in Paris

Where you stay depends on what you're looking for. I always recommend staying in neighborhoods that feel residential while still being central.

Le Marais – great mix of medieval charm and modern energy. You're close to major sights but the neighborhood has its own character with excellent shops, cafés, and restaurants.

Saint-Germain-des-Prés – classic Paris on the Left Bank. Quieter than Le Marais, close to Luxembourg Gardens and Musée d'Orsay. This is where I stay for longer trips.

Canal Saint-Martin – where younger Parisians actually hang out. More residential feel, great brunch spots, interesting bars. A bit further from tourist sites but easy metro access.

For budget stays – look at Belleville or the 11th arrondissement. Good metro connections and genuine neighborhood vibes.

Where to Eat

Skip restaurants near major tourist sites with photos on menus. The best meals happen at neighborhood bistros where locals eat.

Breakfast – grab a pain au chocolat and café crème from the nearest boulangerie. Du Pain et des Idées in the 10th and Mamiche in the 9th are worth seeking out.

Lunch – many restaurants offer fixed-price lunch menus (formules) that cost significantly less than dinner. This is when I splurge on nicer places.

Dinner – book ahead for places like Septime or Le Chateaubriand. For something more spontaneous, wine bars like Le Verre Volé serve excellent food without reservations. The natural wine scene in Paris is exceptional.

Coffee – specialty coffee has finally arrived in Paris. Belleville Brûlerie, Café Lomi, and Boot Café all serve great espresso.

What to Do

The classics – yes, visit the Eiffel Tower. I prefer viewing it from Trocadéro at sunset rather than going up. The Louvre is incredible but plan your visit—three hours is enough to see highlights. Musée d'Orsay is equally impressive and less overwhelming.

Walk everywhere – Paris reveals itself on foot. Cross different Seine bridges, wander through the covered passages (Galerie Vivienne is beautiful), explore the Latin Quarter.

Visit a market – Marché Bastille (Thursday and Sunday) has everything from produce to vintage clothes. Marché des Enfants Rouges in Le Marais is Paris's oldest covered market.

Views – Montparnasse Tower has better views than the Eiffel Tower (and includes the Eiffel Tower in the skyline). Sacré-Cœur in Montmartre offers free sunset views.

A Few of My Favorite Spots

  • Jardin du Luxembourg – perfect for an afternoon with a book
  • Promenade Plantée – elevated park for a quiet walk
  • Shakespeare and Company – iconic English bookshop
  • Merci – beautiful concept store in Le Marais
  • Musée Rodin – sculpture gardens without Louvre crowds
  • Le Bon Marché food hall – great for food souvenirs
  • Parc des Buttes-Chaumont – local park with sunset views
  • Rue des Martyrs – excellent street for food shopping

Practical Information

Transportation – buy a carnet of 10 metro tickets or get a Navigo pass for longer stays. The metro goes everywhere. Walking between stops is often just as fast.

Tipping – service is included in your bill. No need to tip, though rounding up is appreciated.

Best time to visit – spring (April-May) or fall (September-October) for pleasant weather and fewer crowds. August sees many restaurants closed and locals on vacation.

Language – learning basic French (bonjour, merci, s'il vous plaît) goes a long way. Many Parisians speak English but attempting French first is appreciated.

Museums – many offer free admission first Sunday of each month. Book Louvre tickets online and enter through Porte des Lions to avoid lines.
