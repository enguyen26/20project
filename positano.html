Positano Travel Guide

Amalfi Coast, Campania, Italy • Updated January 2025

Positano is one of the most beautiful places I've ever seen. The pastel buildings cascade down the cliffside to a pebble beach where the Mediterranean glows impossibly blue.

I won't sugarcoat it—this isn't a budget destination. Hotels are expensive, restaurants even more so. But I think the setting justifies the cost. Wake up early before day-trippers arrive and watch morning light hit those colorful buildings. You'll understand why people keep coming back.

Where to Stay in Positano

Location matters here more than most places. Positano is built vertically—where you stay determines how many stairs you climb each day.

Near the beach puts you close to restaurants and ferries. Hotels like Le Sirenuse are stunning but expensive. Smaller family-run places offer the same convenience at lower prices.

Upper village hotels offer better value and quieter nights. You're trading convenience for peace—and climbing more stairs. Many hotels provide shuttle service to the beach.

For budget stays—consider Praiano, 15 minutes away. You'll pay half the price and can visit Positano during the day.

Where to Eat

Skip restaurants near the main beach with photos on menus. The best meals are at smaller spots where locals eat.

For seafood—Next2 serves fresh fish without the tourist feel. Da Adolfo beach club is worth the boat ride for grilled fish and a relaxed atmosphere.

For aperitivo—Franco's Bar has the best sunset views. I always get there early to watch the village light up as the sun sets.

For breakfast—skip hotel breakfast. Grab cornetti and cappuccino from small bakeries on side streets.

What to Do

Path of the Gods—this is why you came to the Amalfi Coast. The trail from Bomerano to Nocelle offers stunning views. I recommend doing it in the morning before heat and crowds. It's a moderate 3-4 hour hike.

Take a boat trip—seeing Positano from the water is essential. You can rent a private boat or take a ferry to Capri or Amalfi.

Beaches—Spiaggia Grande is the main beach. It's crowded and sun loungers are expensive, but it's convenient. For something quieter, I prefer Fornillo Beach around the headland.

Church of Santa Maria Assunta—the ceramic dome is beautiful. The Byzantine icon inside is worth seeing if you're passing by.

A Few of My Favorite Spots

  • Path of the Gods trail for coastal views
  • Franco's Bar for sunset aperitivo
  • Fornillo Beach for quieter beach time
  • Early morning walks before 9am
  • Ferry to Capri for day trips
  • Side streets away from the main path

Practical Information

Getting there—from Naples airport, take the Circumvesuviana train to Sorrento, then a SITA bus to Positano. The coastal drive is beautiful but the road winds a lot.

Getting around—walking is your main option. The local bus helps avoid stairs but runs infrequently. Ferries connect to other Amalfi Coast towns.

When to visit—May and September offer the best weather without peak crowds. I avoid August when beaches are packed. October is beautiful if you don't need beach weather.

Money—cash is useful for smaller shops and beach clubs. Many restaurants take cards but confirm before ordering.

Luggage—pack light. The stairs are challenging with heavy bags. Request a hotel porter if you need help.

Crowds—day-trippers arrive between 10am-5pm. Early morning and evening are more peaceful.
