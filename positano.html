Positano Travel Guide

Amalfi Coast, Campania, Italy • Updated January 2025

Positano clings to a clifftop like it's defying gravity, all pastel-colored buildings cascading down to a pebble beach where the Mediterranean stretches endlessly blue. It's undeniably beautiful—arguably the most photogenic village on the Amalfi Coast—and it knows it. The crowds come for the postcard views, but if you time it right and know where to look, you'll find moments of peace among the terraced lemon groves and hidden staircases.

This isn't a budget destination. Everything costs more here than it should—restaurants, hotels, even a simple cappuccino. But the setting justifies it. Wake up early before the day-trippers arrive, watch the morning light hit the colorful buildings, and you'll understand why people keep coming back despite the prices and the steps. So many steps.

Where to Stay in Positano

Location matters more in Positano than almost anywhere else. The village is built vertically, and where you stay determines how many stairs you'll climb daily (spoiler: it's a lot regardless).

Near the beach puts you close to restaurants, ferry docks, and the main action, but expect noise and premium prices. Hotels like Le Sirenuse are iconic for a reason—stunning views, perfect service, and a pool that appears in every Instagram feed. If that's outside your budget, smaller family-run places in this area still give you the convenience without the €800/night price tag.

Upper village accommodations offer better value and quieter nights. You're trading convenience for tranquility (and a serious leg workout). Many places provide shuttle service to the beach, which helps with luggage and tired legs after a day of exploring.

Budget option: Stay in Praiano (15 minutes away) where you'll find half the prices and none of the crowds. You can easily visit Positano for the day without paying the premium to sleep there.

Where to Eat

Positano's dining scene is hit or miss. Tourist-trap restaurants with mediocre food charge premium prices for the view. But there are gems if you look past the obvious choices.

For seafood: Next2 serves fresh fish without the tourist-menu feel. Smaller portions, better quality, and locals actually eat here. Da Adolfo beach club (accessible by boat) is worth the trip for grilled fish and a laidback vibe you won't find in town.

For aperitivo: Franco's Bar has the best sunset views without requiring dinner reservations. Get there early, claim a table, and watch the entire village light up as the sun sets. The spritz is overpriced but you're paying for the view.

For pizza: Surprisingly, Positano isn't known for pizza. If you want excellent Neapolitan pizza, you're better off making a trip to Naples (it's worth it).

For breakfast: Skip hotel breakfast and grab cornetti and cappuccino from small bakeries up the side streets. A quarter of the price and twice as authentic.

What to Do

Walk the Path of the Gods. This is why you came to the Amalfi Coast. The trail from Bomerano to Nocelle offers views that make every tourist photo look like a screensaver. It's a moderate 3-4 hour hike, best done in the morning before heat and crowds. Finish in Nocelle and take the steps down to Positano (or take a bus if your knees are smarter than your ambitions).

Take a boat trip. Seeing Positano from the water is essential. Either rent a private boat (expensive but worth it) or hop on a ferry to Capri or Amalfi. The coastal views are better appreciated from sea level than from the winding roads above.

Explore the beaches. Spiaggia Grande is the main beach—crowded, expensive sun loungers, but convenient. For something quieter, walk to Fornillo Beach around the headland. Fewer amenities but more breathing room and locals swimming instead of posing.

Church of Santa Maria Assunta. The ceramic dome is iconic, and the Byzantine icon inside is supposedly miraculous. Skip it if you're churched-out from the rest of Italy, but it's worth a quick visit if you're passing by.

My Favorite Spots

  • Path of the Gods trail for unbeatable coastal views
  • Franco's Bar for sunset aperitivo
  • Fornillo Beach for a quieter beach day
  • Early morning walks through empty streets before 9am
  • Ferry to Capri for a day trip from the sea
  • Side streets and staircases away from Via Positanesi d'America

Practical Tips

Getting there: From Naples airport, take the Circumvesuviana train to Sorrento, then a SITA bus to Positano. Or splurge on a private car transfer to avoid the stress of public transport with luggage. The coastal drive is beautiful but nauseating if you're prone to motion sickness—sit on the right side for better views.

Getting around: Walking is your only real option within Positano. The local bus helps avoid stairs but runs infrequently. Ferries connect you to other Amalfi Coast towns and are more pleasant than the crowded coastal road.

When to visit: May and September offer the best weather without peak summer crowds. Avoid August unless you enjoy sardine-packed beaches and €50 pasta. October is lovely if you don't need beach weather—fewer tourists, lower prices, still beautiful.

Money: Cash is useful for smaller shops and beach clubs. Many restaurants take cards but always confirm before ordering. ATMs exist but plan ahead since they can run out during peak season.

Luggage: Seriously consider how much you're bringing. Those stairs are no joke, and watching tourists struggle with roller bags on narrow stepped streets is a daily entertainment. Pack light or request a hotel porter.

Crowds: Day-trippers flood in between 10am-5pm. Early morning and evening offer the best light for photos and the most peaceful atmosphere. The village is magical when you have it mostly to yourself.
